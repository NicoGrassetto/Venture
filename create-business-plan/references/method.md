# Business-plan synthesis and review

## Start from the decision

Identify who will read the plan and what they must decide: whether to conduct
research, run a pilot, allocate resources, seek financing, or expand. Do not
assume every plan is a fundraising document. Match the horizon, detail, and
evidence threshold to that decision.

Use the chapter skills to produce inputs, not to fill a predetermined number
of pages. Record an explicit rationale when a method is not applicable.
An early-stage plan can be complete as a decision document while the venture's
critical assumptions remain untested.

## Synthesis map

| Plan section | Inputs and checks |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | Summarize the supported decision, constraints, strongest evidence, and largest remaining uncertainty; write last |
| Problem and customer | Segmentation, beachhead, end-user profile, persona, and customer observations |
| Market opportunity | Bottom-up beachhead sizing, market boundaries, accessible demand, and conditional follow-on markets |
| Solution and value proposition | Life-cycle use case, product specification, quantified customer outcomes, and minimum viable business product |
| Competition and defensibility | Current alternatives, customer purchasing priorities, competitive position, and the venture's core |
| Business model and pricing | Payer, charging unit, payment timing, price range, margin, and willingness-to-pay evidence |
| Go-to-market and sales | Customer decision-making unit, acquisition journey, sales cycle, channels, and fully loaded acquisition costs |
| Operations and team | Founder capabilities, missing ownership, delivery capacity, partners, costs, and jurisdiction-specific professional review needs |
| Financial plan | Cohort-consistent LTV and acquisition costs, revenue drivers, costs, cash movement, scenario assumptions, and funding needs |
| Validation and milestones | Ranked assumptions, precommitted experiments, consumption evidence, product roadmap, owners, and decision gates |
| Risks and assumptions | Venture-killing uncertainties, contrary evidence, dependencies, mitigations, and stop or pivot conditions |
| Evidence and decisions | Evidence IDs, source links, calculation artifacts, review findings, limitations, and revision history |

## Financial consistency

- State currency, units, periods, geography, customer definition, and forecast
  horizon. Keep the same conventions across the plan and its source models.
- Separate observed results from forecasts. Source each material input or
  label it as an assumption with an evidence ID.
- Show a reproducible calculation artifact or explicit formulas and inputs.
  Reconcile customer counts, purchase frequency, price, revenue, gross profit,
  operating expenses, cash flow, and opening/closing cash where applicable.
- Model conservative, base, and upside cases by changing named drivers, not
  by applying an unexplained percentage to the final result.
- Keep contribution-based LTV separate from acquisition cost. Align cohorts
  and periods; account for retention, fulfillment, support, and payment timing.
- Do not confuse revenue, profit, and cash. Explain working capital, capital
  expenditures, financing, and tax assumptions when they matter. Seek qualified
  advice rather than inventing jurisdiction-specific rules.
- Trace funding needs to milestones and a cash forecast. Explain the expected
  action if demand, pricing, retention, costs, or financing differ from plan.

## Separate review pass

Review the plan from the intended reader's perspective after drafting it.
Inspect source material and recalculate the most consequential figures rather
than trusting polished prose or a structural check. Record the reviewer,
date, findings, and disposition in the business-plan task's verification field.

Score each dimension from 0 to 2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision fit | Audience or decision missing | Defined but weakly served | Scope and recommendation directly support the decision |
| Evidence integrity | Invented or untraceable claims | Traceable but important gaps are vague | Material claims are sourced or labeled assumptions; contradictions are addressed |
| Coherence | Sections disagree | Minor unresolved inconsistencies | Customer, offering, economics, resources, and roadmap agree |
| Financial reasoning | Unreproducible or misleading numbers | Transparent model with unresolved checks | Units and scenarios reconcile; material calculations were checked |
| Actionability | No owners or decision gates | Actions exist but are not testable | Highest-risk unknowns have owners, tests, thresholds, and dates |
| Handoff quality | State cannot be resumed | Partial state or review record | Artifacts, checks, limits, and next action are durable and consistent |

For `review-ready`, require at least 10/12, no zero, and resolution of every
material factual or arithmetic error. Evidence integrity must score 2.
Record the dimension scores and unresolved limitations, not just a total.
This rubric evaluates the usefulness and honesty of the plan, not whether its
business assumptions are already validated.

Do not lower the bar after seeing a weak result. If a dimension cannot be
assessed, mark the review incomplete and preserve a draft or blocked state.
For investment, borrowing, regulated activities, or binding commitments,
obtain an appropriately qualified human review before external use.

## Stop and revisit

Reopen upstream decisions when new evidence changes the beachhead, customer
definition, buying process, value proposition, economics, or delivery
constraints. Mark affected tasks and their dependents unfinished. Preserve the
old rationale so a new session can understand why the plan changed.
