# Market Segmentation: Method Reference

Use this reference when the core workflow requires deeper analysis. Keep the final artifact concise and move raw evidence to linked notes or data files.

## Decision objective

A broad but evidence-backed map of market segments, each defined by a specific end user, application, and common buying context.

## Method notes

- Use primary research as the default evidence source. Secondary reports can suggest candidates but cannot validate behavior or buying dynamics.
- Apply the same segment boundary test repeatedly: common need, common product, common sales process, and credible word of mouth.
- Record negative and contradictory evidence; it often reveals that a proposed segment actually contains multiple markets.

## Review questions

- Can the team demonstrate: segments are defined by end user plus application, not broad industries.
- Can the team demonstrate: each priority segment includes direct customer evidence.
- Can the team demonstrate: members within a segment share product needs and buying dynamics.
- Can the team demonstrate: the shortlist preserves multiple credible options without selecting the beachhead prematurely.

Also ask:

- What observation would make the current conclusion wrong?
- Which input has the greatest leverage on the decision?
- Is the evidence from the actual beachhead and actual decision makers?
- Have incentives, selection bias, and survivorship bias been considered?
- Is the next action the cheapest action that can materially reduce uncertainty?
