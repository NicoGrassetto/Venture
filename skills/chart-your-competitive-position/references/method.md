# Chart Your Competitive Position: Method Reference

Use this reference when the core workflow requires deeper analysis. Keep the final artifact concise and move raw evidence to linked notes or data files.

## Decision objective

An evidence-backed competitive position chart and positioning narrative centered on customer priorities rather than vendor-selected features.

## Method notes

- Define behavioral anchors for each axis so different reviewers would place alternatives similarly.
- Use ranges or confidence markers when customer evidence is sparse.
- Treat a weak top-right claim as a product strategy problem, not a graphic design problem.

## Review questions

- Can the team demonstrate: axes are the persona's top priorities and are meaningfully distinct.
- Can the team demonstrate: the status quo is included.
- Can the team demonstrate: placements have evidence or visible uncertainty.
- Can the team demonstrate: the chart implies a real customer choice, not merely a marketing claim.

Also ask:

- What observation would make the current conclusion wrong?
- Which input has the greatest leverage on the decision?
- Is the evidence from the actual beachhead and actual decision makers?
- Have incentives, selection bias, and survivorship bias been considered?
- Is the next action the cheapest action that can materially reduce uncertainty?
